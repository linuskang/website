# hey!

Just wanted to blog this moment because why not... I ended up winning the Open Platform category of the Premier's Coding Competition for 2026!

I was thinking I might share my experiences and some tips in case you feel like participating.

## So what is the competition?

Every year, Education Queensland has this coding competition, and each year has a different topic that you need to centre your app, website, or game on.

For the 2 years that I participated in (2025 & 2026), the topics were: **#digitalinnovationqld** and **Online Safety QLD**. You can find more information about the challenge [here](https://education.qld.gov.au/about-us/events-awards/awards-competitions/premiers-coding-challenge)

## What did you make for 2026?

Ever since the under 16s social media ban has come into effect, I haven't seen any positive benefits from it, many people still haven't been banned, if not already finding ways around it. This brings me to the question of:
 
What are we actually achieving by restricting teenagers from using social media until they turn 16?
 
I believe that restricting access to social media for teenagers is the wrong approach. After all, the government is adding legislation because teenagers aren't safe online and can't use them responsibly. Shouldn't we be teaching them how to use it properly first?
 
So, I built Quacky. It is essentially a school hosted social media platform designed to educate teens about responsible social media usage. It contains with all the social features you would expect from such a platform (including warm fuzzies, daily checkins, memeland, etc), providing institutions with a fully private, invite-only environment within their school community. 
 
Teachers can upload quizzes, lessons, and quests for students to complete on the platform, and in return, students unlock more features (i.e. DMs, posting, commenting, profile features etc..) and coins, which can be used to purchase items in the shop. 
 
Students can also use Quacky as a regular social platform. Everything is moderated to be age appropriate, and there are built in safety features to ensure teenagers are protected. The experience is supposed to educate teens on social media usage by learning through play before they turn 16 and experience it in real life through apps like Instagram.
 
tl;dr schools host the platform themselves, onboard students from year 7-10, allow them to try out a social media platform in an isolated school environment, learn how to use it responsibly, how to report inappropriate content, and earn rewards through it, before they are allowed onto the real platforms.
 
The app is at https://quacky.space, sadly it is invite-only for the timebeing.

Devlogs are public [here](https://quacky.space/devlog)
